1. Introduction

In pharmacovigilance, adverse drug reactions (ADRs) are evaluated according to their seriousness and severity. These terms have different meanings and are important for determining the appropriate safety reporting requirements.

Severity describes the intensity of an adverse reaction, such as mild, moderate, or severe.

Seriousness is a regulatory classification based on the outcome or consequences of the event, such as death, life-threatening conditions, hospitalization, disability, or congenital abnormalities.

A severe reaction is not automatically a serious adverse event. Similarly, a reaction of moderate intensity can be serious if it results in hospitalization.

3. Regulatory Seriousness Criteria

An adverse event is considered serious if it results in one or more of the following:

1. Death: The adverse event results in the patient's death.
2. Life-threatening condition: The patient was at immediate risk of death at the time of the event.
3. Hospitalization: Requires inpatient hospitalization or prolongation of existing hospitalization.
4. Disability: Results in persistent or significant disability or incapacity.
5. Congenital anomaly: Results in a birth defect or congenital abnormality.
6. Other medically important events: Events that may jeopardize the patient or require medical intervention to prevent one of the outcomes listed above.

4. Fictional Patient Case Scenarios

Case 1: Mild Nausea Following Antibiotic Use

A 28-year-old female patient is prescribed amoxicillin for a bacterial infection. After taking the medicine, she experiences mild nausea. The symptoms resolve without additional medical treatment, and she continues normal daily activities.

Assessment:

- Reaction: Nausea
- Severity: Mild
- Hospitalization: No
- Life-threatening: No
- Disability: No
- Outcome: Recovered

Classification: NON-SERIOUS

The reaction is mild and does not meet any regulatory seriousness criterion.

Case 2: Severe Headache Following Medication

A 40-year-old male patient taking a prescribed medication develops a severe headache, rated 9/10 in intensity. He visits an outpatient clinic, receives symptomatic treatment, and is discharged on the same day. No life-threatening condition or other seriousness criterion is documented.

Assessment:

- Reaction: Headache
- Severity: Severe
- Hospitalization: No
- Life-threatening: No
- Disability: No
- Outcome: Recovered

Classification: NON-SERIOUS

Although the headache is severe in intensity, it does not meet the regulatory criteria for a serious adverse event in the fictional scenario.

Case 3: Severe Bronchospasm Requiring Hospitalization

A 52-year-old female patient receiving a prescribed medicine develops acute bronchospasm with significant breathing difficulty. She requires emergency medical intervention and inpatient hospitalization for monitoring and treatment.

Assessment:

- Reaction: Bronchospasm
- Severity: Severe
- Hospitalization: Yes
- Life-threatening: Not established in the available narrative
- Disability: No
- Outcome: Recovered after treatment

Classification: SERIOUS

The event is serious because it required inpatient hospitalization. Seriousness is established even without documented immediate life-threatening status.

Case 4: Suspected Drug-Associated Myocardial Infarction Resulting in Death

A 65-year-old male patient receiving a hypothetical investigational medicine develops acute chest pain and is diagnosed with myocardial infarction. Despite emergency medical intervention, the patient dies.

Assessment:

- Reaction: Myocardial infarction
- Severity: Severe
- Hospitalization: Yes
- Death: Yes
- Outcome: Fatal

Classification: SERIOUS

The event meets the seriousness criteria because it resulted in death. The temporal association does not independently prove that the investigational medicine caused the event.

Case 5: Moderate Skin Rash Following Medication

A 32-year-old female patient develops a moderate skin rash after starting a prescribed medicine. She visits an outpatient clinic, receives treatment, and recovers. No inpatient hospitalization, life-threatening condition, disability, or other serious outcome is reported.

Assessment:

- Reaction: Skin rash
- Severity: Moderate
- Hospitalization: No
- Life-threatening: No
- Disability: No
- Outcome: Recovered

Classification: NON-SERIOUS

The reaction is moderate in intensity but does not meet any reported regulatory seriousness criterion.

7. ICH E2A Expedited Reporting Timelines

Under the ICH E2A framework for clinical trial safety reporting, suspected unexpected serious adverse reactions (SUSARs) are subject to expedited reporting requirements.

Type of event| Reporting timeline
Unexpected fatal or life-threatening suspected adverse reaction| Within 7 calendar days of initial receipt
Other serious and unexpected suspected adverse reactions| Within 15 calendar days
Follow-up information for an initially reported fatal or life-threatening case| As soon as possible, with the additional information required by the applicable framework

For the traditional ICH E2A framework, the initial 7-day report for fatal or life-threatening SUSARs is followed by a further 8-day period for a more complete report when necessary.

Important: These are clinical-trial expedited reporting timelines, not universal deadlines for every adverse event. Actual requirements depend on the applicable national regulations, product type, and reporting context. A non-serious event is not automatically subject to expedited SUSAR reporting.

8. Difference Between Severity and Seriousness

Severity| Seriousness
Describes intensity of symptoms| Describes regulatory consequences
Mild, moderate, severe| Serious or non-serious
Based on clinical intensity| Based on defined outcome criteria
Does not determine reporting status by itself| Helps determine regulatory reporting requirements

9. Conclusion

The classification of adverse drug reactions is an essential pharmacovigilance activity. It helps identify important safety events and determine the appropriate reporting pathway.

The five fictional cases demonstrate that severity and seriousness must be evaluated independently. Regulatory criteria such as death, life-threatening conditions, hospitalization, disability, and congenital abnormalities are central to seriousness assessment.

Knowledge of ICH E2A also helps pharmacovigilance professionals understand expedited safety reporting requirements in clinical development.
